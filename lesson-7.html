Verb patterns

Common patterns when one verb follows another
PatternVerbs and phrasesExamples
Gerund: verb + -ingavoid, discuss, dislike, don’t mind, enjoy, feel like, keep, practice, quit, suggestI enjoy cooking.
We discussed moving house.
Infinitive: to + verbagree, choose, decide, expect, hope, learn, need, seem, want, wish, would likeWe agreed to meet.
I’d like to join you.
Either formbegin, can’t stand, continue, hate, like, love, prefer, start, tryI like walking.
I like to walk.

With the other verbs in “Either form”, both forms can work. Sometimes the focus changes. Use to + verb after would like: “I’d like to go.”

Same verb, different meaning

With these four verbs, the form changes the meaning.

Stop

Stop + -ing

End an activity.

We stopped talking when the film began.

We were talking, and then we became quiet.

Stop + to + verb

Pause one activity to do something else.

We stopped to talk to a friend on our way home.

We were walking home. We paused because we wanted to talk.

Try

Try + -ing

Test an idea to see if it helps.

I tried drinking less coffee, and I slept better.

I changed my habit to see if it helped me sleep.

Try + to + verb

Make an effort to do something.

I tried to open the jar, but the lid was too tight.

I made an effort, but I could not open it. “Try to” alone does not tell us if the action was successful.

Remember

Remember + -ing

Have a memory of a past action.

I remember locking the door before I left.

I locked it, and now I can remember doing it.

Remember + to + verb

Remember a task and then do it.

I remembered to lock the door before I left.

I did not forget the task, so I locked the door.

Forget

Forget + -ing

Not remember a past action or experience.

I’ll never forget meeting my best friend on my first day at school.

We met in the past. Because I say “never forget”, I mean I will always remember it.

Forget + to + verb

Not do a task because you do not remember it.

I forgot to call Mia yesterday.

I planned to call her, but I did not make the call.
